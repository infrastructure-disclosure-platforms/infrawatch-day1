Testing whether a public project pays for itself

Net present value (NPV) compares what the public pays for an infrastructure project with what it gets back over the project’s life, with future money pulled back to what it is worth today. Enter what the record shows and read the result as a policy message, not a yes or no.

1

Money today is worth more than money tomorrow. A shilling spent now could have earned interest, so a shilling returned in ten years is worth less than one returned today. NPV accounts for that gap with a discount rate.

2

Discount the future, then compare. Pull every future cost and benefit back to today’s value, add them up, and set the total against what the public paid up front. If the discounted benefits clear the costs, the project pays for itself.

3

The answer is only as honest as the disclosure. NPV runs on the financing terms, costs and revenues that are actually disclosed. Where the loan terms are sealed, the test cannot see the poison. The limit is disclosure, not the maths.
