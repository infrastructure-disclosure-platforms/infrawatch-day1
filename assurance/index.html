What the record says, read together

Disclosure rises toward 100%. Accountability does not follow.

The number that goes up is publication. The thing that was supposed to go up with it, a public that can see, question, and act on how its infrastructure money is spent, stays flat. Across the assurance reports, the binding constraint is no longer whether data is published. It is whether the data is reachable and legible to the people it is for.

Before you read on

Make the call yourself

When a country pushes its infrastructure disclosure up to ~90%, how high do you think public awareness and use of that data climbs with it? Set your guess.

50%

The reports do not show a matching climb. Honduras at 98.1% disclosure records the public "unaware of the portals". Uganda notes data sits on donor platforms "which may not be known by the public". Malawi finds the information "not easily understandable to the general public". High publication, low reach, repeatedly. Your guess assumed the two move together. The record says they decouple.

Prefer not to interact? The finding holds without the slider: across the corpus, publication and public reach move apart.

Four findings only the whole corpus shows

What repeats across borders

PATTERN 01

The overruns start upstream, in preparation, not at the tender

Country after country, the driver of cost and time overruns is not corruption at the bid. It is inadequate project preparation and design that has to be redone during construction.

"All projects studied required substantial redesign during execution due to lack of exhaustive prior review." (Honduras Fifth). Echoed in Malawi 2019 ("inadequate Project Preparation") and Uganda Third ("scope changes and poor planning").
PATTERN 02

Institutions answer when asked, but do not publish by default

Reactive disclosure consistently outruns proactive disclosure. The data exists; releasing it still depends on someone requesting it.

Uganda Fourth: reactive 70% vs proactive 55%. Malawi 2019: proactive just 8% to 11%. Ghana Sekondi-Takoradi: proactive 89.5% vs reactive 54.8%.
PATTERN 03

Who funds the project predicts how transparent it is

A resource-driven integrity gap runs through the record: externally financed projects carry the specialists and the scrutiny; nationally financed ones often do not.

"Externally funded projects benefit from experienced specialists, while national projects lack budget for such roles." (Honduras Fifth). Uganda First records disclosure swinging from 37.8% to 84.4% with the funding source.
PATTERN 04

Where law gives way to politics, the numbers move hardest

The sharpest failures are not technical. They are where political pressure overrides the procurement rules the assurance process exists to check.

Malawi 2018 (LUANAR): procurement advice overridden by "political expedience", cost escalation over 300%.
What the reports recommend, in their own words

The fix the record keeps reaching for

If the gap is reach, not publication, the reports converge on closing it: put the data where people are, in a form they can use, backed by law.

"The Office of the Prime Minister should strengthen the use of community Barazas for citizens to build trust in Government's work."
CoST Uganda Second Assurance Report · recommendation
"Mainstream disclosure into the policy framework and the Access to Information Law."
Minister of Works and Transport, as recorded in the CoST Uganda Second Assurance Report

Open data portals, community meetings that read the data aloud, and a legal duty to publish: the same three moves, in different countries, aimed at the same gap.

Method and limits

Source: 92 CoST independent assurance reports and related publications, crawled from infrastructuretransparency.org and read with a structured extraction model. Figures (disclosure rates, gaps, escalation) are quoted as reported in each document; they are not re-measured here and should be confirmed against the source before external citation. Three reports of the discovered reports were all retrieved and parsed. This essay reads patterns across the corpus; individual report methodologies differ across countries and years, so cross-country figures are directional, not strictly comparable. No figure on this page was invented; where a report stated no rate, none is shown.
